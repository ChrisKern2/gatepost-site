The Thesis

Good companies, growing communities, and a better way to grow together

Pest control is a service families and businesses rely on year in and year out. Across the Southeast, much of that work is done by family and founder-owned companies with loyal customers, good technicians, and names their communities trust. Many of them have grown as far as word of mouth and the owner's own hours will take them.

That is the opportunity. A partnership of neighboring companies, sharing marketing, systems, and people, can grow faster together than any of them could alone, while keeping the local names and faces customers already know.

An essential service

Homes and businesses need pest control in good years and lean ones. It is a need, not a luxury, and demand keeps coming back.

Relationships that recur

Customers stay on a regular plan with a technician they know. Those relationships are the heart of every good local company, and the partnership protects them.

Route density drives margin

A technician with more stops on the same streets earns far more per day at nearly the same cost. Neighboring companies that combine routes get there fastest.

A growing Southeast

New households, warm and humid seasons that keep getting longer, and suburbs full of owner-occupied homes. Raleigh–Durham, coastal Carolina, Richmond, and Tennessee lead the list.

Partnership

Row together, go further

A rowing crew is faster than its strongest rower, because everyone pulls on the same stroke. That is how this partnership gets built. Owners who join are not bought out and shown the door. Every one of them rolls equity into the combined company, keeps leading the people who know them if they want to, and shares in what the business becomes.

  1. 01

    The founding company

    One well-run pest control business becomes the base. Chris steps in full-time as its operator, the team and the name stay, and the owner rolls part of the proceeds into the platform as the first partner.

  2. 02

    Neighbors join

    Operators whose routes sit next to ours join as partners. Their technicians pick up stops on the same streets, and their customers keep seeing the same faces.

  3. 03

    Grow together

    Shared marketing, shared systems, and shared buying power compound across every branch. Every partner owns a piece of a larger, more valuable regional company.

Investment Criteria

A founding company, and the partners who join it

Building one company for the long run changes what matters in a sale. Cash at closing, the name and the crew stay, real ownership in what comes next, and a straight answer quickly when the fit is wrong. Here is what fits.

Size

Established, profitable companies with a track record. The founding company typically has $500,000 to $3,000,000 of EBITDA, and partner companies can be smaller operators near our routes.

Pest Control

Residential-led service with recurring quarterly or monthly plans, ideally mixed with local commercial accounts. Termite, mosquito, and lawn work fit alongside a recurring base.

Southeast

North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia, and Tennessee. Growing suburbs with owner-occupied homes fit best, led by Raleigh–Durham, Wilmington to Myrtle Beach, and Richmond.

Partnership Structure

Gatepost Partners buys a majority interest, and every owner rolls equity into the combined company as a partner. A licensed operator stays on through the transition. A day-to-day role for you is welcomed but never required.

A Good Fit

  • An owner planning succession who wants to share in what the partnership builds next
  • 10 or more years in business, with a name people in the community recognize
  • Most revenue from recurring service agreements, with steady customer retention
  • A licensed operator or lead technician who plans to stay and grow with the partnership
  • Tax returns, a profit and loss statement, and a customer list. That is enough to start.

Out of Scope

  • Start-ups, pre-revenue companies, and turnarounds
  • Founding companies below $500,000 or above $3,000,000 of EBITDA
  • Minority positions, since the purchase is a majority interest
  • A full cash exit with no continuing ownership in the partnership
  • Books built mainly on one-time jobs, new-construction pretreats, or national accounts
  • Locations outside the states listed above

How This Works

What you can hold Gatepost to

Choosing a partner for the company you built is a slow, private decision, and you will make it once. These are the rules Gatepost Partners holds itself to while you think it over.

Straight Answers
If the business is not a fit, you hear so on the first call, with the reason. You should not learn that from silence 6 weeks later.
Confidentiality
A confidentiality agreement is signed before you send anything. Your employees, customers, and competitors will not hear about it from Gatepost Partners.
Certainty of Close
Equity is committed before the letter of intent is signed. Debt is arranged during exclusivity, with lenders lined up in advance.
Continuity
The name stays, the phone number stays, and the crew keeps their jobs and their routes. Customers keep seeing the technician they already know.
Aligned Interests
Every owner who joins rolls equity alongside Gatepost into the same company. When the platform grows, every partner who helped build it shares in it.

The Process

Start with a conversation about what you want next

From the first call to closing usually takes 3 to 4 months. You will still be running routes the whole time, so the work stays on this side of the table wherever possible. The partnership starts on the day after closing.

  1. 1

    Introductory call

    30 minutes

    A call about the company, what you want to happen next, and how a partnership would work for you. A confidentiality agreement is signed first.

  2. 2

    In-person meeting

    2 to 3 hours

    A visit to the office to meet the people who run the work, ride along on a route, and talk through how your company would fit in the platform.

  3. 3

    Letter of intent

    2 to 3 weeks

    Price, structure, your partnership rollover, and your role after closing, in plain English. Equity is committed before signing, so the terms hold.

  4. 4

    Due diligence

    8 to 12 weeks

    A review of financials, customer retention, routes, licensing, and termite history, run around your schedule. Your team hears about it when you decide to tell them.

  5. 5

    Close

    2 to 3 weeks

    Documents are signed and funds move. You are paid for your majority stake, and your rollover becomes your ownership in the combined company. The partnership begins.

  6. 6

    Row together

    The years ahead

    The platform's marketing, systems, and people get to work on your routes, neighboring companies join, and the business you started keeps growing as part of something larger.
